## Key Takeaways

- AGI lacks agreed-upon definitions — experts can't even agree whether animals are intelligent or conscious, making 'artificial general intelligence' a meaningless target with no finish line.
- LLMs fail at real coding not because they lack 'general intelligence' but because they can't hold the full context of a codebase — its history, business logic, human politics, and architectural decisions. This is the gap that LLMOps, harness engineering, and multi-agent orchestration aim to bridge.
- Specialisation beats generality for most real-world tasks — a senior engineer who knows your codebase for 10 years outperforms a brilliant generalist every time. The same principle applies to AI.
- LLMs are a statistical trick, not intelligence — adding more parameters to a lie produces a bigger lie, not a truth. No amount of scaling fixes the fundamental approximation problem.
- The real Turing test is whether CEOs are gullible enough to replace their workforce with a tool that only performs well inside its training distribution.
- Stop extrapolating from AI progress and evaluate the technology only on what it demonstrably does today — everything else is hype, fear, and propaganda. Judge the tool, not the roadmap.
